Client context

The client is a multi-site NHS GP group with three sites. Each has a reception team handling the patient line and a steady flow of incoming clinical letters.

The manual process and its cost

Reception teams across all three sites were overwhelmed by call volume. The group was planning two more receptionist hires to cope.

At the same time, every incoming clinical letter was read, coded and filed by hand into eleven different workflow queues.

What we built

  • Sophia, a voice agent on the patient line using a structured five-tier urgency triage framework, integrated with the practice clinical system, with call summaries filed to the patient record
  • An AI letter classifier with a clinical coding pipeline using SNOMED CT
  • A staff approval portal: people approve every classification before it is filed

How we tested it

Nothing went near a patient until staff were happy with it.

  1. Alpha: we observed the real workflow with the practice's own workflow team.
  2. Beta: we built the routing rules from how experienced staff actually file letters.
  3. Build: internal staff testing before any patient exposure.
  4. Omega: a phased rollout, starting with straightforward letters.

Return on investment

The cost of the problem
Two planned receptionist hires across three sites, and every clinical letter read and filed by hand into eleven queues.
Measured return
Around £60,000 a year of reception time freed, and around £30,000 a year projected per practice from the letter classifier.
Payback
Inside the first year.

Results

  • Reception time freed and redeployed, avoiding two planned receptionist hires, worth around £60,000 a year across three sites
  • The letter classifier is projected to save around £30,000 a year per practice
  • Clinical coding accuracy trending upward through supervised use

What changed operationally

  • The patient line answers every call, and call summaries land in the patient record
  • Reception staff spend less time on the phone and more on work that needs a person
  • Staff approve letter classifications instead of reading, coding and filing each one from scratch
  • Nothing reaches a patient record without human approval
  • Built around NHS assurance requirements from day one
