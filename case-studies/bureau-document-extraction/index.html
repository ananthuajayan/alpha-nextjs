Client context

The client is an Irish document management and scanning bureau. It scans and indexes documents, then delivers the data in an Access-based import format that downstream systems depend on.

The manual process and its cost

The bureau indexed around 25,000 scanned pages a month by hand. That took 3 to 3.5 full-time staff doing data entry, at 18 to 60 seconds per document. Between 5 and 10% of the work had to be redone.

Volumes were growing 15 to 20% a year, so the problem was getting bigger. Whatever replaced the manual work had to deliver output in the bureau's existing import format, unchanged.

What we built

  • An AI extraction pipeline with a visual validation layer and multi-prompt self-correction
  • Automatic routing of low-confidence documents to human review
  • Results returned in the client's exact import format over secure SFTP
  • All processing EU-resident, in Dublin

The accuracy trial

We didn't ask the bureau to take accuracy on trust. We measured it on the client's own documents, against ground truth, before they committed.

  1. Alpha: a compatibility test on a sample batch of the bureau's own documents.
  2. Beta: a ground-truth accuracy trial on 4,177 hospital proof-of-delivery documents, including handwritten reference numbers. We measured every field against verified answers.
  3. Build and Omega: a production engagement, now running at volume.

Return on investment

The cost of the problem
3 to 3.5 full-time staff keying data from 25,000 pages a month, with 5 to 10% rework and volumes growing 15 to 20% a year.
Measured return
86.5% of documents fully correct first time in a ground-truth trial, and 98.3% processed without review in a 7,055-document batch.

Results

On the ground-truth batch of 4,177 documents:

  • 97.9% accuracy on purchase order numbers
  • 96.3% accuracy on handwritten goods-received numbers
  • 86.5% of documents fully correct, with the rest automatically flagged for review

A second batch of 7,055 documents ran with only 1.7% flagged for human review, so 98.3% went through without anyone touching them. The platform handles up to around 20,000 documents a day.

What this means if you run a bureau

Your team stops keying every document and only checks the fields the system flags. The output arrives in your existing import format, so nothing downstream changes. It runs white-label, so your client stays your client. And you see the accuracy on your own documents before you commit.

What changed operationally

  • Staff check a small review queue instead of keying every document by hand
  • Downstream systems carried on exactly as before, with the same import format
  • Data stays in the EU, in Dublin
  • The review queue is built in and visible, so the bureau always knows what needs a person
